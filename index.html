First, what is ChatGPT? GPT stands for Generative Pre-trained Transformer. It is a conversational AI assistant from OpenAI, built on a Large Language Model that generates human-like text. You can chat with it, get advice, research, write code, solve complex math problems, or brainstorm. You reach it through a website, an app, or an API. And who uses it? Students, developers, marketers, businessmen, writers and ordinary people, every day. For many of them it has become irreplaceable. [0:40]

Test one: is ChatGPT an invention? Let's go back to 2017. A Google Research team published a paper called "Attention Is All You Need" and introduced the Transformer (Vaswani et al., 2017). Before that, language models used RNNs, recurrent neural networks. They read text word by word. So what was the problem? They were slow, very expensive to train, and they struggled with long texts. What did the Transformer change? It replaced recurrence with parallel processing on GPUs, so much bigger and stronger models became possible. How does it work? Text becomes tokens, then vectors. Self-attention weighs every word against the others. A neural network processes it, and the output vectors are decoded back into words. [CLICK] So yes, it is an invention. But in 2017 it was only an innovative idea. It was not yet an innovation. [1:10]

From invention to innovation

November 2022

  • OpenAI releases ChatGPT
  • Built on LLMs using the Transformer
  • One change: decoder-only
  • Zero learning curve, unlike Excel or Photoshop

A chat window. That is all.

So what turned the idea into an innovation? In November 2022, OpenAI released ChatGPT. It is built on large language models using the Transformer architecture, with a few modifications, such as the decoder-only approach. And here is the key. Think about Excel or Photoshop. You need time to learn them. ChatGPT? It is a simple chat window. A zero learning curve. Yet it can help with an email, a report, a piece of code, or your homework, instantly. Everyday problems suddenly became easier to solve. That is why people noticed it. [0:45]

But wait. Chatbots already existed. Alexa. Siri. Why was that not a revolution? Because they were based on intent recognition, decision trees and pattern matching (Adamopoulou & Moussiades, 2020). Step outside the script, and you hear: "Sorry, I don't understand." ChatGPT understands nuance, tone and language without any if-else scripts. Old chatbots did one specific task. ChatGPT is a general-purpose tool. And it does not just retrieve raw data. It is generative: it writes an original answer for your request, based on its training and sometimes on retrieved data through RAG, Retrieval-Augmented Generation. [0:50]

There are four contextual factors. First, technology. Besides Transformers, there was the hidden potential of GPUs. GPUs were always great at parallel work, but in 2012, at the ImageNet challenge, Alex Krizhevsky used CUDA to run a neural network directly on two GTX 580 GPUs. His project, AlexNet, reached a top-5 error rate of 15.3 percent. An astonishing result (Krizhevsky et al., 2012). Second, economy. OpenAI started in 2015 as a non-profit, funded by donors like Sam Altman and Elon Musk with one billion dollars (OpenAI, 2015). By 2019 that was not enough to train transformers. So Microsoft financed the compute with another billion dollars, mostly in Azure credits. And around the viral launch in 2022 and 2023, it was reported to invest ten times more (Bloomberg News, 2023). Society and culture
  • Employers see productivity gains

Politics and institutions

Third, social and cultural factors. Employers saw the productivity gains. So using ChatGPT and Codex was not only endorsed, but sometimes required, especially in software development (Shibu, 2025). Students found a personal coach, a private tutor and an assistant (Freeman, 2025). Fourth, political and institutional factors. These were controversial. Supportive frameworks, ethical guidelines and regulations bring stability and a privacy-first approach. Uzbekistan, for example, strongly supports AI with its 2030 integration plan (Kruger, 2025). But Italy temporarily banned ChatGPT (Al Jazeera, 2023). OpenAI responded with understanding, took the required measures, and the ban was removed. [0:50]

So who are the stakeholders? The innovators: OpenAI's researchers and founders, and the Google researchers who developed Transformers. The investor: Microsoft, the most prominent one. Then those who decide. Governments create regulations. Schools and universities decide whether students may use it. Employers decide whether employees use AI at work. And finally, the users themselves. They choose whether to use it at all. [0:35]

Winners and losers

Who benefits
  • Students: help with studies
  • Developers: more productive coding
  • Teachers: content for lessons
  • Marketers and copywriters: creative content
Who loses
  • Junior and entry-level jobs: higher standards, AI does it faster (Brynjolfsson et al., 2025)
  • Creative writers and copywriters: same reason (Hui et al., 2024)
  • Schools: cheating lowers education quality (Cotton et al., 2024)

The group that loses is not smaller.

So who wins, and who loses? Winners: students, developers, teachers, marketers, copywriters. But the group that loses is not smaller. Junior and entry-level jobs, mostly in software development. Standards rise, and employers prefer AI that does the same job in much less time (Brynjolfsson et al., 2025). Creative writers and copywriters are affected for the same reason (Hui et al., 2024). And schools, because students use AI to cheat on exams and assignments, which lowers the quality of education (Cotton et al., 2024). [0:45]

Bottom-up
Last question. Who pushed this innovation: the top or the bottom? The answer is both. Top-down came first: capital and infrastructure from OpenAI and Microsoft, cloud compute, the Google researchers, and government regulation. Then bottom-up. Millions of everyday users, students, educators, marketers, businessmen, reached one hundred million a week (Malik, 2023). They suggest improvements, becoming decentralized co-authors, and they spread it by word of mouth and social media. In fact, people started using AI tools before their employers endorsed them (Microsoft, 2024). The initiative came from people themselves. [0:45]

Our verdict

Invention? Yes: the Transformer.

Widely adopted? Yes: 100 million weekly users (Malik, 2023)

Changed habits? Yes: across professions and everyday life.

Therefore: ChatGPT is an innovation.

Costs: pressure on junior jobs, cheating in education. Used appropriately, it is a useful, productive tool.

So, back to our three tests. [CLICK] Is ChatGPT an invention? Yes: the Transformer. [CLICK] Is it widely adopted? Yes: one hundred million weekly users (Malik, 2023). [CLICK] Did it change habits and practices? Yes: across professions and everyday life. [CLICK] Therefore, by our definition, ChatGPT is an innovation. It has negative effects: higher expectations for junior employees, and cheating in education. But used appropriately, it is a useful and productive tool. [0:35]
