(≈1:00) Let's start with one question. What is an innovation? Our course definition says: [READ THE TEXTBOOK DEFINITION HERE]. In simple words, it is a new or significantly changed way of doing something, which people adopt, and which changes their habits and routines. Notice that it does not say "a new technology". So we picked two cases that test this idea from opposite sides. ChatGPT: technology, a brand-new invention, started by big companies. And virtual museums: art and culture, no new technology at all, started by artists, students and activists. Our question is simple. Do they mean the same thing by innovation? And who drives each one, the top or the bottom?

Case 1 · Technology

Case 1: ChatGPT

  • Sector: technology, artificial intelligence
  • Launched November 2022 by OpenAI
  • Conversational assistant on a Large Language Model
  • 100 million weekly users by November 2023 (Malik, 2023)

Why chosen: a new technology that became a daily habit.

MakersOpenAI and Google researchers

InvestorMicrosoft

UsersStudents, developers, marketers, managers, everyday users

(Malik, 2023) 3
(≈0:45) Case one: ChatGPT. It is a conversational AI assistant from OpenAI, built on a Large Language Model. The sector is technology, specifically artificial intelligence. The main actors are OpenAI and its researchers, the Google researchers who created the Transformer, and Microsoft as the key investor. The users are students, developers, marketers, managers and ordinary people. Governments, schools and employers also decide whether it is used. Less than a year after its launch in November 2022, it reached one hundred million weekly active users (Malik, 2023). We chose it because it is the clearest example of a new technology becoming a daily habit.

4
(≈0:50) So what does innovation mean here? [CLICK] Step one: invention. In 2017, Google researchers introduced the Transformer, which replaced slow word-by-word models with parallel processing (Vaswani et al., 2017). But an invention is only an idea. [CLICK] Step two: adoption. One hundred million weekly users (Malik, 2023). [CLICK] Step three: changed routines. ChatGPT has a zero learning curve, just one chat window. And unlike older chatbots such as Siri and Alexa, which relied on scripts and pattern matching, it is generative and general-purpose (Adamopoulou & Moussiades, 2020). [CLICK] So for ChatGPT, innovation means a new technology adopted at massive scale.

5
(≈0:45) Why did this happen in 2022? Four factors. Technology: the Transformer, plus the power of GPUs. Economy: OpenAI began as a non-profit, but training these models cost far more, and Microsoft reportedly invested ten billion dollars (Bloomberg News, 2023). Society: employers saw productivity gains, and in some companies using AI tools became required (Shibu, 2025). And politics: governments set the rules, and they responded very differently. So the context was computing power, big money and employer demand.

Case 1 · Direction

ChatGPT: top-down created it, bottom-up spread it

Top-down
Bottom-up
1 · Created Capital, cloud compute and research from OpenAI, Microsoft, Google researchers.
2 · Spread Users spread it by word of mouth and suggested improvements. People used AI before employers endorsed it (Microsoft, 2024)

6
(≈0:45) Now, top-down or bottom-up? [CLICK] ChatGPT was created top-down. Capital, cloud computing and research came from OpenAI, Microsoft and the Google researchers. [CLICK] But it spread bottom-up. Users shared it by word of mouth and social media and suggested improvements. In fact, employees started using AI tools before their employers endorsed them (Microsoft, 2024). [CLICK] Then the top came back: some employers made AI tools a requirement (Shibu, 2025). So top-down created it, bottom-up spread it, and top-down formalised it.

Case 1 · Influence

ChatGPT: influence on society and access

Access gained

  • Almost anyone with a browser gets help
  • No training needed
  • Students, developers, teachers, marketers benefit

Pressure created

  • Junior and entry-level jobs under pressure (Brynjolfsson et al., 2025)
  • Cheating lowers education quality (Cotton et al., 2024)

Wider access to capability, higher pressure on workers and schools.

7
(≈0:45) What did ChatGPT change in society? It widened access. Almost anyone with a browser can get help with writing, coding or studying, with no training. Students, developers, teachers and marketers benefit. But there are costs. Junior and entry-level jobs are under pressure, because employers prefer AI that does the same work faster (Brynjolfsson et al., 2025). And schools face cheating, which lowers the quality of education (Cotton et al., 2024). So ChatGPT widened access to capability, and raised pressure on workers and schools.

(Bishara, 2020), (Century, 2021) 8
(≈0:55) Case two: virtual museums and independent exhibitions in Minecraft and Roblox. The context is the pandemic. In 2020, nearly ninety percent of the world's museums, more than 85,000, closed their doors (Bishara, 2020). Artists, students and curators moved culture into games. The sector is art, culture and media. The actors are independent artists, students, NGOs, museums, brands, and the platform owners. The audience is mostly young players, but also readers in countries where news is censored. Examples include the Uncensored Library in Minecraft, the São Paulo Museum of Modern Art in Minecraft (Century, 2021), and Gucci Garden in Roblox. We chose it because it is culture, not business, and it needs no new technology.

Case 2 · Meaning

Virtual museums: innovation = a new use, not a new tool

Before Museum = a building you visit.
After Museum = a world you enter as an avatar, and can build yourself.
“The most accessible, the most easy to use” (Smith, n.d.)

Artist Brad Downey on why he chose Minecraft

Meaning: a significantly changed way of making and visiting culture.

(Smith, n.d.) 9
(≈0:45) What does innovation mean here? Nothing was invented. Minecraft and Roblox already existed. The innovation is a significantly changed way of showing and visiting culture. [CLICK] A museum is no longer only a building. [CLICK] It is a world you enter as an avatar, and can even build yourself. [CLICK] The artist Brad Downey chose Minecraft because it is, in his words, "the most accessible, the most easy to use" (Smith, n.d.). [CLICK] So here, innovation is not a new tool. It is a new use. And it changes habits. In the Roblox study, students did not just visit a museum, they built one (Kang et al., 2022). Audiences become players, and curators become builders.

Case 2 · Context

Why 2020? Crisis, easy tools, censorship

Crisis

Closed museums needed new ways to reach people.

(Bishara, 2020)

Easy tools

15 students in 4 teams built a virtual art museum in Roblox Studio.

(Kang et al., 2022)

Censorship

Banned journalism stored as in-game books where Minecraft stays accessible.

(Peet, n.d.)

Context = crisis + cheap tools + a young audience.

10
(≈0:50) Why did it appear in 2020? Three reasons. First, crisis: closed museums needed new ways to reach people (Bishara, 2020). Second, easy tools: in one study, fifteen students in four teams built a virtual art museum in Roblox Studio (Kang et al., 2022). Third, censorship: the Uncensored Library stores banned journalism as in-game books, inside a game that stays accessible in many countries (Peet, n.d.). So the context was a crisis, cheap tools and a young audience.

Case 2 · Direction

Virtual museums: bottom-up started it, top-down scaled it

Top-down
Bottom-up
1 · Started
  • Artists and students built open spaces, even an “anarchy mode” where visitors changed the works (Smith, n.d.)
  • Uncensored Library: 24 builders from 16 countries, 7 months, partners pro bono (Peet, n.d.)
2 · Scaled
  • MAM São Paulo teamed up with Minecraft (Century, 2021)
  • Gucci Garden on Roblox: 19.9 million visits in under 14 days (Roblox, n.d.)

Platform owners set the rules for everyone.

11
(≈0:55) Top-down or bottom-up? Here, bottom-up came first. [CLICK] Independent artists and students built open spaces. One Minecraft exhibition even had an "anarchy mode", where visitors could change the works (Smith, n.d.). The Uncensored Library was built by twenty-four builders from sixteen countries over seven months, with partners working pro bono (Peet, n.d.). [CLICK] Then institutions joined. The São Paulo museum teamed up with Minecraft (Century, 2021), and Gucci built Gucci Garden in Roblox, with almost twenty million visits in under two weeks (Roblox, n.d.). [CLICK] And the platform owners always set the rules. So bottom-up started it, and top-down scaled it.

Case 2 · Influence

Virtual museums: influence and limits

Access

Banned articles reach readers in censored countries.

(Peet, n.d.)

Participation

Students and visitors build and change art, not only look at it.

(Kang et al., 2022; Smith, n.d.)

Limits

Only 5% of museums in Africa and small island states could go online; half of humanity lacks digital access.

(Bishara, 2020)
12
(≈0:50) What did it change? Access: banned articles reach readers in censored countries (Peet, n.d.). Participation: students and visitors do not only look at art, they build it and change it (Kang et al., 2022; Smith, n.d.). Gucci Garden shows another side: visitors acquired 1.2 million virtual items (Roblox, n.d.). Culture became something you wear and collect, not only watch. But there are limits. Only five percent of museums in Africa and small island states could offer online content, and half of humanity lacks access to digital technology (Bishara, 2020). So virtual museums opened culture to many people, and left others out.

Comparison

Same definition, different engines

Aspect ChatGPT Virtual museums
Sector Technology (AI) Art and culture
Meaning of innovation New technology at mass scale New use of existing tools
Actors Firms, investors, users Artists, students, NGOs, brands
Direction Top-down creation, bottom-up spread Bottom-up creation, top-down scaling
Access effect Wider access, pressure on juniors and schools Wider access, digital divide remains

Innovation is changed practice, not technology. Top-down brings scale. Bottom-up brings meaning and participation.

13
(≈1:15) Now, the comparison. Both cases fit our definition: a changed way of doing things that changes habits. But the meaning is different. For ChatGPT, innovation means a new technology adopted at scale. For virtual museums, it means a new use of existing technology that changes who can make and see culture. The engines are opposite. ChatGPT: top-down creation, bottom-up spread. Virtual museums: bottom-up creation, top-down scaling. And both widened access, while leaving some people out: junior workers and schools in one case, people without digital access in the other. So, to answer our question: they mean different things by innovation, but both follow the same definition. Our conclusion is this. Innovation in society is defined by changed practice, not by technology. Top-down brings scale. Bottom-up brings meaning and participation. The best innovations need both.
